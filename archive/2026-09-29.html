Executive BLUF

Rural Health Transformation implementation accelerated again today. CMS announced $169.6 million across 91 Colorado grantees and roughly 250 projects, plus $51 million for 68 rural Texas hospital districts and authorities focused on chronic disease prevention and management. The awards reinforce a common operating model: regional specialty access, emergency capability, mobile and virtual care, workforce development, chronic-disease infrastructure and services that can be sustained beyond a single facility. Oklahoma also has new rural substance-use funding to act on, with McAlester Regional Health Center Authority receiving a $300,000 RCORP overdose-response award and Cimarron Memorial Hospital & Nursing Home receiving a $100,000 RCORP planning award. Immediate execution remains concentrated on September 30 and October 1 deadlines, especially the RHC telehealth billing conversion.

Top 5 Developments

1

Colorado RHTP award scales regional specialty, emergency and mobile capability

Rural HealthStrategy

CMS announced $169.6 million for 91 Colorado grantees and approximately 250 projects. The portfolio includes telemedicine, mobile care, behavioral and maternal health, workforce initiatives, strengthened emergency services and approaches intended to bring specialty care and blood transfusion capability closer to rural patients.

Why it matters: the scale and project mix provide another concrete example of transformation capital being used to build distributed capability across regions rather than simply replicate every service at every site.
Executive question: Which capabilities in Oklahoma would create the greatest access improvement if deployed regionally rather than facility by facility?
2

Texas directs $51 million through rural hospital districts and authorities for chronic disease

RHTPPopulation Health

CMS announced $51 million for 68 rural Texas hospital districts and authorities to create or improve community-based prevention, wellness and nutrition programs for diabetes, cardiovascular disease, chronic respiratory disease and obesity.

Why it matters: RHTP is moving beyond episodic hospital care into chronic-disease infrastructure that can connect RHCs, hospitals and community partners. Sustainability will depend on whether these services ultimately have reimbursement, value-based savings or durable operating support.
Executive question: Which Oklahoma chronic-disease capabilities could transition from grant-supported programming into reimbursable or value-based models?
3

HRSA rural addiction awards create two new Oklahoma access points

OklahomaBehavioral Health

HRSA awarded $89.3 million nationally through the Rural Communities Opioid Response Program. Oklahoma recipients include McAlester Regional Health Center Authority with a $300,000 overdose-response award and Cimarron Memorial Hospital & Nursing Home with a $100,000 planning award. The Peoria Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma also received a $100,000 planning award.

Why it matters: the awards create practical rural treatment and recovery infrastructure that can complement medication access, RHC behavioral-health services, emergency care and referral pathways.
Executive question: Where can rural hospitals and RHCs connect into these funded networks instead of building duplicative OUD capacity?
4

Oklahoma remains aligned with CMS shift toward outcomes-based Medicaid quality

OklahomaMedicaid

CMS's Investing in Health Outcomes initiative now includes 37 state partners and emphasizes health outcomes, fewer duplicative measures, digital quality measurement and stronger alignment between financial accountability and results. Oklahoma publicly supports the direction.

Why it matters: this could influence future SoonerSelect quality strategies, procurements and value-based arrangements while raising the importance of rural attribution, interoperability and timely data.
Executive question: Which current rural quality measures and data workflows would be hardest to convert into near-real-time outcomes reporting?
5

RHC telehealth billing conversion is now a final operational readiness issue

OklahomaAction

Beginning October 1, RHCs and FQHCs furnishing distant-site telehealth transition from G2025 to individual CPT or HCPCS codes, with modifier 93 for qualifying audio-only services and modifier 95 for synchronous audio-video services when applicable.

Why it matters: the remaining risk is operational. Documentation, charge capture, coding, claim edits, clearinghouse configuration and payer-specific rules all need to align to avoid preventable denials.
Executive question: Can every affected RHC demonstrate a clean end-to-end test claim by September 30?
