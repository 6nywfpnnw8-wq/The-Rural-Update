Executive BLUF

Implementation risk is now the priority. The RHC/FQHC telehealth conversion is in its second day, making first-claim performance more important than readiness attestations. Federal Medicaid and CHIP eligibility restrictions for several categories of lawfully present immigrants also became effective October 1, creating a new payer-mix and uncompensated-care watch. Alabama announced a second RHTP grant round of nearly $55 million across 34 projects, reinforcing the shift toward state portfolios of rural capabilities. Oklahoma remains in active RHTP implementation, and Medicare's first AI-enabled CT New Technology Add-on Payment is now an important signal that selected clinical AI can move into defined reimbursement pathways.

Executive Actions

Top 5 Developments

1

RHC telehealth enters production monitoring

ActionRevenue Cycle

The October 1 conversion from G2025 to individual CPT/HCPCS service codes is live. Modifier 93 applies to qualifying audio-only services and modifier 95 to synchronous audio-video services when applicable.

Why it matters: configuration defects can now show up as real denials and delayed cash.
Executive question: Are first claims clearing correctly across traditional Medicare, Medicare Advantage and commercial payers?
2

Medicaid eligibility restrictions create a new coverage watch

MedicaidFinance

Federal Medicaid and CHIP eligibility restrictions for several categories of lawfully present immigrants became effective October 1 under the 2025 reconciliation law. Emergency Medicaid remains available for qualifying emergency services.

Why it matters: coverage loss can shift encounters toward self-pay and uncompensated care, with uneven local effects.
Executive question: Is October payer mix being measured against a September baseline without using immigration status as a proxy?
3

Alabama adds nearly $55 million in second-round RHTP grants

Rural HealthRHTP

Alabama announced 34 additional Rural Health Transformation grants totaling nearly $55 million after awarding more than $144 million in its first round. Projects span access, maternal health, EMS, cancer screening, workforce and other rural capabilities.

Why it matters: RHTP is increasingly functioning as a portfolio strategy, not a series of isolated grants.
Executive question: Are portfolio dependencies, duplication and recurring costs visible before projects become permanent operating commitments?
4

Oklahoma RHTP moves from awards to execution

OklahomaRural Health

Oklahoma's recent progress report emphasizes first-year implementation and CMS spending milestones. SWOSU is deploying mobile screening, chronic-disease prevention, telehealth and wellness resources in Washita and Roger Mills counties.

Why it matters: the management question is now whether temporary capital produces durable access and measurable outcomes.
Executive question: Does each major project have an accountable owner, milestone, outcome and post-grant operating plan?
5

Medicare payment begins to recognize selected clinical AI

AIMedicare

CMS approved New Technology Add-on Payment eligibility for an AI-enabled CT multi-triage technology for qualifying inpatient use, creating a defined Medicare payment pathway for a specific clinical AI capability.

Why it matters: clinical-AI evaluation increasingly needs reimbursement analysis alongside clinical evidence, cybersecurity, interoperability and total cost.
Executive question: Is reimbursement status part of the AI procurement and governance checklist?
