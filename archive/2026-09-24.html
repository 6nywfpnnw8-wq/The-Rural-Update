Executive BLUF

Oklahoma now has seven Rural Emergency Hospitals. Ascension St. John Nowata's completed conversion preserves 24/7 emergency and outpatient care but ends local acute inpatient and swing-bed services, making transfer capacity, EMS burden and post-acute access the immediate policy questions. Nationally, rural workforce risk increased as the administration renewed a $100,000 H-1B payment requirement for certain new petitions while DHS separately proposes a $103,265 fee for cap-subject petitions. AHA is seeking a healthcare exemption, citing disproportionate effects on rural and underserved hospitals. RHTP implementation is also moving into measurable productivity: West Virginia's first Provider Productivity Support Fund award combines an AI contact center, documentation tools, chronic care management and remote monitoring. The October 1 RHC telehealth billing conversion remains the clearest near-term operational deadline.

Executive Actions

Sep 30
Validate RHC telehealth conversion. Require a clean end-to-end test claim, modifier validation and staff readiness before October 1.
Q4

Q4

Top 5 Developments

1

Nowata becomes Oklahoma's seventh Rural Emergency Hospital

OklahomaStrategy

Ascension St. John Nowata is now operating as a Rural Emergency Hospital. The model preserves 24/7 emergency care and outpatient radiology and laboratory services, but acute inpatient and swing-bed services are no longer available locally. StateImpact reports Nowata is Oklahoma's seventh REH and the 56th nationally as of September 23.

Why it matters: the conversion illustrates the central REH tradeoff. The community keeps a hospital access point, but inpatient and skilled post-acute capability shift elsewhere. That can move pressure onto transfers, EMS availability, receiving hospitals and families.
Executive question: What transfer, EMS and post-acute capacity must surround an REH so that preserved emergency access does not create a new downstream access problem?
2

H-1B policy creates a new rural workforce cost and recruitment risk

WorkforceFederal Policy

The White House renewed for another year a $100,000 payment requirement for certain new H-1B petitions involving workers entering the United States. Separately, DHS has proposed a $103,265 fee for cap-subject H-1B petitions. On September 23, AHA urged DHS to exempt healthcare workers and protect foreign-trained physicians using Conrad 30 pathways.

Why it matters: rural hospitals already recruit from thinner physician, nursing and laboratory labor pools. Large new petition costs can make international recruitment uneconomic for small facilities even when a candidate is otherwise viable.
Executive question: Which rural vacancies and physician recruitment pipelines depend on H-1B or Conrad 30 pathways, and what would those positions cost under each policy scenario?
3

RHTP moves from technology grants to measurable provider productivity

Rural HealthAI & Technology

West Virginia's first Provider Productivity Support Fund award will finance a 24/7 AI-powered patient contact center, AI clinical documentation tools, expanded Chronic Care Management and Remote Patient Monitoring for diabetes and hypertension. The $500,000 award is part of a broader state RHTP initiative designed to reduce administrative burden and improve provider efficiency.

Why it matters: this is a useful RHTP test case because the investment can be evaluated against operational outcomes: staff time, appointment access, CCM enrollment, RPM utilization and downstream reimbursement. It also forces the sustainability question because software and staffing costs continue after grant funding.
Executive question: Which productivity investments can demonstrate both measurable labor savings and a recurring revenue or expense-offset model by 2031?
4

RHC telehealth conversion is one week away

Revenue CycleAction

On October 1, RHCs and FQHCs furnishing distant-site telehealth move from G2025 to individual CPT or HCPCS service codes, using modifier 93 for qualifying audio-only services and modifier 95 for synchronous audio-video services.

Why it matters: readiness now needs to be demonstrated through a clean claim, not staff education alone. Documentation, charge capture, coding, edits, clearinghouse transmission and payer acceptance all need to function.
Executive question: Can every RHC produce a clean end-to-end test claim before September 30?
5

Healthcare AI procurement expands beyond clinical performance and cybersecurity

AI & TechnologyGovernance

Health Care Without Harm released a U.S. AI procurement guide for healthcare organizations that adds environmental and infrastructure disclosure questions to vendor evaluation. The framework asks buyers to assess issues such as data-center energy, water, pollution and community impacts alongside the broader governance questions already emerging around validation, security and oversight.

Why it matters: AI procurement is becoming a multidimensional governance function. Hospitals increasingly need to understand not only what a model does, but also where it runs, what infrastructure it depends on, what data it uses and what external costs or concentration risks accompany the service.
Executive question: Does the AI procurement process collect enough information to evaluate clinical value, security, vendor concentration, infrastructure dependence and total operating impact?
