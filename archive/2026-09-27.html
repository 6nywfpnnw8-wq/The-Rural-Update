Executive BLUF

CMS has launched a 37-state Medicaid quality initiative that shifts emphasis toward outcomes, streamlined measures, digital quality measurement and financial accountability. Oklahoma is publicly aligned with the effort, making future Medicaid managed-care procurement and quality strategy worth watching closely. HRSA's FY2026 Rural Maternity and Obstetrics Management Strategies cohort includes Duncan Regional Hospital, giving Oklahoma a new federally supported rural maternal-health network opportunity. Rural Health Transformation implementation also continues to clarify the national direction: mobile care, remote monitoring, workforce pipelines, regional hubs, behavioral health and infrastructure modernization are recurring themes across recent state awards. The immediate operational deadline remains October 1, when RHC and FQHC distant-site telehealth billing transitions from G2025 to individual service codes.

Top 5 Developments

1

CMS launches 37-state Medicaid quality partnership

OklahomaMedicaid

CMS launched Investing in Health Outcomes with 37 state partners. The voluntary pledge emphasizes outcomes over process, a smaller measure inventory, digital quality measurement and stronger linkage between financial accountability and results. Oklahoma is among the public supporters of the initiative.

Why it matters: the direction is consistent with a broader move toward outcome-based Medicaid accountability and may influence future managed-care quality strategies and procurements.
Executive question: Which SoonerSelect measures, data capabilities and rural-provider workflows would need to change if Oklahoma adopts the pledge principles in operational form?
2

Duncan Regional joins FY2026 federal rural maternity cohort

OklahomaMaternal Health

HRSA lists Duncan Regional Hospital among seven FY2026 Rural Maternity and Obstetrics Management Strategies award recipients. RMOMS is designed around regional risk-appropriate care, coordinated maternal-care networks, telehealth and specialty access, and financial sustainability.

Why it matters: Oklahoma gains a new federally supported rural maternal-health network that may create referral, workforce, telehealth and care-coordination opportunities beyond the lead organization.
Executive question: Where could rural hospitals and RHCs plug into the network to improve prenatal, delivery, postpartum or specialty access?
3

RHTP awards reinforce regional and distributed rural delivery models

Rural HealthStrategy

Recent CMS awards in Arkansas, Delaware and South Dakota continue the pattern of pairing workforce development with mobile care, remote monitoring, chronic-disease management, behavioral health and infrastructure modernization.

Why it matters: transformation funding is increasingly building networks and distributed capabilities rather than simply preserving every legacy service at every site.
Executive question: Which investments have a recurring reimbursement, labor-savings or value-based pathway once federal grant funding ends?
4

RHC telehealth billing conversion enters final readiness window

Revenue CycleAction

On October 1, RHCs and FQHCs furnishing distant-site telehealth move from G2025 to individual CPT or HCPCS service codes, including modifier 93 for qualifying audio-only services and modifier 95 for synchronous audio-video services when applicable.

Why it matters: any mismatch across documentation, coding, charge capture, claim edits or payer configuration can create avoidable denials.
Executive question: Can every affected RHC demonstrate a clean end-to-end claim before September 30?
5

Oklahoma REH experience keeps transfer and post-acute capacity in focus

OklahomaAccess

Ascension St. John Nowata is operating as a Rural Emergency Hospital, preserving 24/7 emergency and outpatient access while no longer offering acute inpatient or swing-bed services locally.

Why it matters: REH sustainability cannot be evaluated only at the facility level. Transfer performance, EMS availability, receiving capacity and post-acute access become part of the access model.
Executive question: What regional metrics should Oklahoma use to evaluate whether REH conversion preserves access without creating new downstream gaps?
