        rhc and fqhc distant-site telehealth billing conversion (cms transmittal r13776otn)

        Revenue cycle and MedicareFinal

        CMS issued Transmittal R13776OTN to require RHCs and FQHCs to bill distant-site telehealth services using individual CPT/HCPCS codes plus applicable telehealth modifiers rather than the single HCPCS code G2025. CMS’s FQHC/RHC PFS announcement states this policy will begin October 1, 2026; the transmittal document (R13776OTN) lists an Implementation Date of October 5, 2026 and notes Provider Education was released May 27, 2026. Operationally, teams should treat the PFS language and the transmittal as complementary but distinct: the PFS language describes the programmatic effective date, while the transmittal identifies the implementation date for transmittal-level processing and CR-related changes. Revenue-cycle and payer-testing plans must therefore include a focused test window for dates of service October 1–October 5 (inclusive) to determine whether payers accepted CPT/HCPCS plus telehealth modifiers before or only on/after the transmittal’s listed implementation date, and then a broader sample of October production to document remittance patterns and any required corrective actions.

        Why it matters: Billing-system logic, encounter building and EDI edits must be updated immediately. First production claims for October 2026 are likely to surface payer-specific rejections, modifier edits and remittance differences that affect revenue timing and net payment. CAHs that host or bill for RHCs must reconcile claims, remittances and denial reasons to avoid lost revenue or downstream cash-flow effects.
        Executive question: Has revenue-cycle extracted and reconciled a representative sample of October 1–21 RHC distant-site telehealth claims by CPT/HCPCS, telehealth modifier, POS and payer remittance to quantify denials, price differences and cash impact?

        cms-2452-p proposed rule on provider taxes and indirect hold-harmless thresholds

        Federal policy and Medicaid financingProposed

        CMS published NPRM CMS-2452-P implementing statutory limits on health-care-related provider taxes and revising indirect hold-harmless thresholds; the CMS fact sheet references statutory timing that begins Oct 1, 2026 for some provisions and later effective thresholds beginning Oct 1, 2027 for certain classes.

        Why it matters: If finalized as proposed, state supplemental-payment design and provider-tax-funded payments could be constrained, creating material downside to hospital supplemental revenue streams that many rural facilities rely on. Oklahoma exposure depends on final rule language and OHCA policy choices. Finance teams should not rebalance budgets to this NPRM until state-specific terms are verified, but must run downside cash-stress scenarios now.
        Executive question: What is the hospital system's quantifiable revenue and cash-flow exposure to loss or reclassification of state supplemental payments that could arise if CMS finalizes CMS-2452-P as proposed, and what state-level evidence does OHCA provide about intended adjustments?

        3

        cms GENEROUS model: national participation announced; state execution conditional

        Medicaid pharmacy and hospital pharmacy revenueAnnouncement

        CMS announced broad national participation interest in the GENEROUS Medicaid drug-payment model and reported 40 states plus Puerto Rico had signed agreements as of the Sep 18, 2026 press release. The model uses manufacturer supplemental rebates and state agreements to align selected Medicaid drug net prices with international benchmarks.

        Why it matters: GENEROUS can alter Medicaid drug payment flows, manufacturer rebate mechanics and managed-care passthroughs, with potential downstream effects on hospital outpatient pharmacy margins and 340B economics. However, the national announcement does not establish Oklahoma's executed participation terms or product-level rules. Hospitals should request OHCA documentation before changing pharmacy, 340B or revenue forecasts.
        Executive question: Has OHCA executed GENEROUS participation agreements and published covered-drug lists, rebate pass-through rules or MCO operational guidance that would change hospital pharmacy reimbursement or 340B program economics?

        cms rural health transformation program moving to state implementation; oklahoma published nofos and recipients

        Grants and operational sustainabilityMonitoring

        CMS RHTP awards are in state-level implementation. Oklahoma published RHTP program pages, NOFO materials, funding-recipient lists and subrecipient guidance that define contract-execution and spend windows for subawards.

        Why it matters: RHTP grants provide time-limited funds for telehealth, mobile units, workforce and behavioral investments, but they are not ongoing operating revenue. Hospitals participating as subrecipients or partners must document recurring staffing, maintenance and replacement costs and create post-grant sustainability plans before integrating services into the core operating model.
        Executive question: Which Oklahoma RHTP projects involve the hospital as a recipient or partner, what are the subaward execution and spend deadlines, and what concrete post-grant recurring-cost commitments will be required to maintain services after the grant period?

        ohca removes brixadi prior authorization under a value-based agreement; hrsa publishes fy26 rcorp awards

        Behavioral health and Medicaid operationsMonitoring

        OHCA announced removal of prior authorization for Brixadi for SoonerCare members as part of a value-based pharmacy agreement (state press release dated 2026-09-14). Separately, HRSA published FY26 RCORP award recipients and funding that expand rural opioid treatment planning and services.

        Why it matters: Removing prior authorization for Brixadi reduces an administrative barrier and may increase access to long-acting buprenorphine in rural clinics and hospitals, but facilities must confirm billing, dispensing, cold-chain handling and administration reimbursement. RCORP awards present partnership and funding opportunities for treatment and recovery capacity building; hospitals should map recipients and pursue collaboration where appropriate.
        Executive question: Has the hospital confirmed OHCA provider guidance for Brixadi administration, billing codes and reimbursement, and have local RCORP awardees been identified as partners for expanding MAT, recovery services or referral pathways?
