        Medicare finalizes RHC/FQHC distant-site telehealth billing by CPT/HCPCS plus telehealth modifier

        medicare-paymentFinal

        CMS finalized distant-site telehealth billing for RHCs and FQHCs in Transmittal R13776OTN, requiring individual CPT/HCPCS codes plus telehealth modifiers for Medicare FFS. CMS guidance indicates telehealth billing for RHCs/FQHCs will take effect for dates of service beginning October 1, 2026, with the transmittal implementation date listed as October 5, 2026. This converts distant-site telehealth from rate-based aggregate payments to per-service CPT/HCPCS adjudication and creates immediate production-claims and EDI edit risk.

        Why it matters: RHC and FQHC revenue and cash flow depend on payer acceptance and clearinghouse edits. Practical risk is operational: front-end edits, modifier logic, payer-specific acceptance and remittances will determine near-term denials and cash impact. Oklahoma clinics and hospital-affiliated RHCs should reconcile Medicare telehealth dates of service from October 1 onward and track payer responses to quantify real-dollar effects.
        Executive question: Have revenue-cycle teams extracted a representative sample of RHC/FQHC telehealth claims (DOS Oct 1 onward), reconciled CPT/HCPCS and telehealth modifier usage, and logged payer-specific rejections for corrective edits?

        CMS proposes new indirect hold-harmless thresholds (CMS-2452-P) that could change state supplemental-payment constructs

        medicaid-financeProposed

        CMS published NPRM CMS-2452-P to implement statutory changes to the indirect hold-harmless threshold for health care-related taxes. The NPRM reflects statutory provisions with a statute-level effective date of October 1, 2026, but the CMS fact sheet notes a material timing nuance: new indirect hold-harmless thresholds for most permissible classes in States that expanded Medicaid to the adult group begin taking effect on October 1, 2027. Treat the NPRM as a proposed implementation of those statutory tests and review state-specific timing when modeling exposure (CMS fact sheet; src-299e421cf61e682c).

        Why it matters: State supplemental payments and provider-tax-funded support can represent material revenue for rural hospitals. If finalized as proposed, the NPRM could reduce or reprice state supplemental-payment streams that depend on indirect hold-harmless constructs. Hospitals must treat the proposal as a planning risk, isolate provider-tax-funded revenues in downside scenarios, and request state-level exposure information before changing forecasts.
        Executive question: What portion of our Medicaid revenue and cash flow is tied to provider-tax-funded supplemental payments that could be affected if CMS finalizes CMS-2452-P, and has OHCA shared any state exposure analysis?

        CMS announces broad state-level participation in the GENEROUS Medicaid drug-payment model; Oklahoma execution conditional

        pharmacy-policyAnnouncement

        CMS announced that all states applied to participate in the GENEROUS Medicaid drug-payment model and published model details; multiple states have signed participation agreements. The GENEROUS model centralizes negotiations for selected drug prices and expects state operational alignment. Oklahoma-specific executed participation terms were not published in the national announcement.

        Why it matters: GENEROUS could change Medicaid drug net prices, manufacturer supplemental rebates, and state pharmacy operations. For rural hospitals this raises conditional questions about 340B passthroughs, formulary impact and Medicaid reimbursement flows. Oklahoma hospitals should not rebase pharmacy or 340B forecasts until OHCA confirms whether Oklahoma executed GENEROUS participation and provides implementation terms.
        Executive question: Has OHCA executed GENEROUS participation terms, and if so, which products, rebate passthrough rules and MCO workflows will affect our pharmacy and 340B economics?

        Oklahoma RHTP funds a Behavioral Health Relocation Incentive to recruit rural behavioral-health providers

        workforceAnnouncement

        Oklahoma HWTC published a Rural Health Transformation Behavioral Health Relocation Incentive that offers up to $250,000 over five years for licensed behavioral-health providers who relocate to eligible rural Oklahoma communities and commit to five years of service.

        Why it matters: The relocation incentive creates a funded recruitment tool that can help rural hospitals and clinics fill behavioral-health roles, but it binds recipients to multi-year service commitments and requires coordination with local recruitment and credentialing processes. Hospitals should map candidate eligibility and on-boarding timelines against service-commitment terms.
        Executive question: Can our credentialing, payroll and supervision processes onboard relocation-incentive candidates in time to fulfill the program’s service-commitment schedule, and what local supports (office space, telehealth access) will be required?
