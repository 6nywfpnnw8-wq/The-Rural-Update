Executive BLUF

Rural transformation is becoming infrastructure strategy. CMS announced $7.2 million for 25 South Dakota projects connecting rural EMS, ambulance-based telemedicine, hospital data exchange, workforce training and emergency communications. CMS also finalized the mandatory GLOBE Medicare Part B drug model, effective November 30, creating a new pharmacy-finance exposure to map. In Oklahoma, OHCA's RHTP HIE and EHR Assistance application is open through December 31, creating a practical path to improve interoperability, EHR capability and readiness for payer APIs. The operating priority remains disciplined execution: keep the RHC telehealth claims watch active and connect temporary RHTP investments to durable regional capability.

Top 5 Developments

1

South Dakota links rural EMS, telemedicine and hospital data

Rural HealthEMS

CMS announced $7.2 million in RHTP funding for 25 South Dakota projects. Investments include ambulance-based telemedicine, EMS workforce training, patient-data exchange with hospitals, dispatch and communications modernization, and data-driven quality improvement.

Why it matters: regionalization depends on transport and information infrastructure, not hospitals alone.
Executive question: Is EMS capability being modeled alongside hospital service redesign, transfer strategy and bypass analysis?
2

CMS finalizes mandatory GLOBE Part B drug model

PaymentPharmacy

The GLOBE final rule was published October 2 and becomes effective November 30. The model tests an alternative method for calculating Part B drug inflation rebates for certain separately payable drugs and biological products and applies through randomized assignment to 25% of Original Medicare Part B beneficiaries.

Why it matters: high-cost Part B drugs can create material acquisition, reimbursement and cash-flow exposure for rural providers.
Executive question: Which drugs and sites of service create meaningful exposure before the model takes effect?
3

Oklahoma opens HIE and EHR assistance through RHTP

OklahomaTechnology

OHCA is accepting Year One applications through December 31 for eligible rural providers seeking statewide HIE participation and assistance implementing, replacing, optimizing or expanding certified EHR systems.

Why it matters: interoperability is becoming prerequisite infrastructure for referral integration, electronic prior authorization, payer APIs, quality measurement and value-based care.
Executive question: Which affiliates have technology gaps that should be matched to this assistance before year end?
4

Rural affiliation continues to shift from management to capability extension

IndustryAffiliation

Tampa General assumed operational control of the former DeSoto Memorial Hospital in rural Florida, pairing local hospital access with system specialty capability, technology investment and advanced transport resources.

Why it matters: affiliation value should be measured by capabilities and capital extended into the rural market, not affiliation status alone.
Executive question: Can each rural partnership quantify the services, expertise, technology and financial capability the larger network adds?
5

Clinical AI governance becomes more operational

AIGovernance

FDA's AI-enabled medical-device list continues to expand across radiology, cardiovascular care, ultrasound, pathology and other specialties, while FDA is exploring clearer identification of devices incorporating foundation models and multimodal systems.

Why it matters: regulated clinical AI requires a different governance pathway from general administrative generative AI.
Executive question: Does procurement distinguish FDA status, intended use, evidence, monitoring, cybersecurity, interoperability and reimbursement?
