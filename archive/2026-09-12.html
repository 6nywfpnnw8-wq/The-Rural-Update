Executive BLUF

The most immediate decision remains the CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule comment deadline on September 14, including a proposed recurring RHC payment pathway for Diabetes Self-Management Training and Medical Nutrition Therapy. Oklahoma's PRIMS school-health deadline has closed, shifting state attention toward January 1 SoonerCare work-requirement readiness and the October 15 ROOTS rural-school wellness opportunity. CMS is also using Rural Health Transformation funding to strengthen rural long-term care capacity in Vermont, while bipartisan Senate negotiators are considering a federal duty-of-care framework for frontier AI that could include authority to block unsafe model releases. These developments point to the same leadership requirement: convert temporary opportunities into sustainable operating models while tightening controls around reimbursement, eligibility, post-acute capacity, and autonomous technology.

Top 5 Developments

1

CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule comments close Monday

MedicareAction

CMS closes the CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule comment period on September 14. The proposal would recognize Diabetes Self-Management Training and Medical Nutrition Therapy as qualified preventive services payable as stand-alone RHC visits through the All-Inclusive Rate. CMS also proposes Shared Savings Program changes intended to strengthen incentives for higher-risk participation.

Why it matters: this is a near-term opportunity to influence both recurring RHC reimbursement and the value-based care environment. The DSMT and MNT proposal is especially important because it offers a path for chronic-disease capabilities to move from grant support or subsidy into ordinary Medicare reimbursement.
Executive question: Which provisions require formal comment before Monday, and which RHCs could operationalize DSMT or MNT if the proposal is finalized?
2

Medicaid financing pressure and Oklahoma coverage churn should be modeled together

OklahomaAction

Comments on CMS-2452-P close September 21. CMS estimates the provider-tax rule would reduce federal Medicaid expenditures by $246 billion from 2026 through 2035, with new thresholds for most permissible provider classes in expansion states beginning October 1, 2027. Separately, OHCA is already notifying SoonerCare expansion adults who may be affected by federal work requirements beginning January 1, 2027.

Why it matters: supplemental-financing pressure and eligibility churn can hit the same hospital from opposite directions. Lower supplemental support can weaken Medicaid economics while coverage losses can increase self-pay conversion, charity care, and bad debt.
Executive question: Which rural facilities have the least capacity to absorb both tighter Medicaid financing and a measurable increase in Medicaid-to-self-pay conversion?
3

RHC telehealth billing conversion is now less than three weeks away

Revenue CycleAction

Beginning October 1, RHCs and FQHCs furnishing distant-site telehealth must stop using G2025 and bill the individual CPT or HCPCS code describing the service, using modifier 93 for qualifying real-time audio-only services and modifier 95 for real-time audio-video services. CMS lists the CY 2026 distant-site telehealth payment rate for RHCs and FQHCs at $97.53.

Why it matters: this is a coding and claim-workflow conversion with direct denial risk. Readiness requires more than clinician education because charge masters, claim edits, documentation guidance, clearinghouses, and payer testing all need to align by October 1.
Executive question: Can every RHC demonstrate an end-to-end test claim under the new individual-code workflow before September 30?
4

Rural Health Transformation is moving into long-term care and post-acute capacity

Rural StrategyNew

CMS announced an $11.7 million Rural Health Transformation investment in Vermont on September 10. Of that amount, $9 million will provide tuition benefits to healthcare professionals who commit to rural service for at least five years, while $2.7 million will support three rural nursing homes and four community partners, including complex-care capacity and Licensed Nursing Assistant training.

Why it matters: this expands the emerging RHTP model beyond hospital walls. Rural sustainability depends on post-acute capacity, workforce stability, and the ability to keep complex patients close to home rather than treating hospital access as an isolated problem.
Executive question: Which rural transformation investments reduce avoidable hospital days or distant post-acute placement by strengthening local nursing, rehabilitation, transportation, or home-based capacity?
5

Federal AI policy is moving toward a legal duty of care for frontier models

AI & CyberNew

Reuters reported September 11 that bipartisan Senate negotiators are considering legislation that would require frontier AI developers to mitigate known major risks under a duty-of-care standard and could authorize the federal government to block release of models judged unsafe, subject to judicial challenge. The proposal remains under negotiation. The House Health Subcommittee separately holds its Medicare provider-payment and healthcare cybersecurity hearing September 15.

Why it matters: AI governance is moving from voluntary safeguards toward potential statutory obligations. Healthcare organizations adopting agentic systems should expect stronger expectations around capability testing, access controls, incident reporting, independent monitoring, and rapid shutdown authority.
Executive question: Are enterprise AI agents governed according to what they can access and execute, with technical controls strong enough to satisfy a future duty-of-care standard?
