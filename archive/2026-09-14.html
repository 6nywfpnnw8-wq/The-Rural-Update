Executive BLUF

The CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule comment period closes today, making the proposed RHC payment pathway for Diabetes Self-Management Training and Medical Nutrition Therapy, physician payment reductions, remote-monitoring policy and Shared Savings Program changes the immediate federal decision point. Tomorrow, the House Energy and Commerce Health Subcommittee takes up Medicare provider payment and healthcare cybersecurity. Medicaid financing remains the largest medium-term rural financial exposure, while Oklahoma prepares for January 1 SoonerCare work requirements. RHC telehealth billing conversion on October 1 remains a direct revenue-cycle risk. Frontier AI cyber capability continues to raise the control standard for healthcare systems that give models tools, credentials or execution authority.

Top 5 Developments

1

CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule comments close today

MedicareAction

CMS closes comments on CMS-1848-P today, September 14. The proposal would recognize DSMT and MNT as stand-alone RHC visits paid through the All-Inclusive Rate. It also proposes 2027 physician conversion factors of $33.17 for qualifying APM participants and $32.84 for non-qualifying participants, both lower than 2026 as the temporary 2.5% statutory increase expires. CMS is also seeking changes to remote monitoring and Shared Savings Program financial methodology.

Why it matters: the rule combines a concrete RHC growth opportunity with physician-payment pressure and value-based-care policy changes. It also proposes that RPM and RTM generally require an initiating visit and clinical staff employed by the practice rather than contractors.
Executive question: Which provisions require formal comment today, and which 2027 operational or financial assumptions need to be revised even if no comment is submitted?
2

Congress takes up Medicare payment and healthcare cybersecurity tomorrow

CongressNew

The House Energy and Commerce Health Subcommittee will hold a September 15 legislative hearing on Medicare provider payment and healthcare cybersecurity. The agenda includes multiple physician-payment bills and proposals aimed at strengthening cyber resilience across the healthcare sector.

Why it matters: rural providers face persistent physician-recruitment economics and disproportionately high per-unit cybersecurity costs. Congressional treatment of both issues in one hearing reinforces that payment stability and cyber resilience are both access-to-care infrastructure questions.
Executive question: Which proposals would materially improve rural physician economics or impose new cybersecurity obligations that should be planned for now?
3

Medicaid financing pressure and Oklahoma coverage churn remain one combined risk

OklahomaAction

Comments on CMS-2452-P close September 21. CMS estimates the provider-tax rule would reduce federal Medicaid expenditures by $246 billion from 2026 through 2035. Oklahoma is separately notifying SoonerCare members about federal work requirements beginning January 1, 2027 for affected adults ages 19 through 64 who are not exempt.

Why it matters: rural hospitals may face reduced supplemental-financing flexibility at the same time that eligibility churn increases Medicaid-to-self-pay conversion, charity care and bad debt. These effects belong in one facility-level downside model.
Executive question: Which rural facilities or essential service lines become financially vulnerable first if both pressures materialize in the same year?
4

RHC telehealth conversion remains an October 1 revenue-cycle deadline

Revenue CycleAction

For dates of service on or after October 1, CMS requires RHCs and FQHCs furnishing distant-site telehealth to bill the individual CPT or HCPCS service code rather than G2025. Modifier 93 applies to qualifying synchronous audio-only services and modifier 95 to synchronous audio-video services.

Why it matters: the change affects charge capture, coding, documentation, claim edits, clearinghouses and payer testing. Preventable denials are likely if any part of the workflow remains configured around G2025.
Executive question: Can every RHC demonstrate a clean end-to-end test claim before September 30?
5

AI cyber capability is shifting from advisory support toward autonomous execution

AI & CyberContinuing

OpenAI reports that GPT-6 Astra meets its Critical cybersecurity capability threshold, meaning that with appropriate tools and access it can identify previously unknown vulnerabilities and develop exploits with substantially less human direction. Anthropic's September threat-intelligence report describes increasingly autonomous AI-enabled cyber operations, including multi-agent reconnaissance, exploitation and data exfiltration.

Why it matters: healthcare organizations should treat AI agents with credentials, network access or execution authority as privileged identities. Autonomy changes the speed and scale of both defensive and offensive cyber activity.
Executive question: Can every enterprise AI agent be uniquely identified, independently logged, isolated and stripped of access without depending on the model or vendor to police itself?
