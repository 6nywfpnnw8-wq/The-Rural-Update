Top 5 Developments

1

CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule comments close tomorrow

MedicareAction

CMS closes the CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule comment period at 11:59 p.m. on September 14. The proposal includes stand-alone RHC payment for Diabetes Self-Management Training and Medical Nutrition Therapy through the All-Inclusive Rate, plus changes affecting telehealth, physician payment, the Ambulatory Specialty Model, and the Medicare Shared Savings Program.

Why it matters: this is the final near-term opportunity to influence a recurring reimbursement pathway for rural chronic-disease services and broader payment policy that will shape 2027 physician and accountable-care economics.
Executive question: Which provisions merit formal comment, and which RHCs could operationalize DSMT or MNT if CMS finalizes the proposal?
2

Medicaid financing pressure and Oklahoma coverage churn remain one combined risk

OklahomaAction

Comments on CMS-2452-P close September 21. CMS estimates the provider-tax rule would reduce federal Medicaid expenditures by $246 billion from 2026 through 2035. Oklahoma is separately preparing for federal work requirements beginning January 1, 2027 for affected SoonerCare expansion adults, with OHCA already notifying members and directing providers to outreach materials.

Why it matters: rural hospitals can face lower supplemental financing and higher uncompensated care at the same time. Treating provider taxes, state-directed payments, DSH, enrollment churn, charity care, and bad debt as separate issues understates the facility-level downside.
Executive question: Which rural facilities have the least financial capacity to absorb both tighter Medicaid financing and higher Medicaid-to-self-pay conversion?
3

RHC telehealth billing conversion is now an October 1 operational deadline

Revenue CycleAction

CMS says RHCs and FQHCs must stop using G2025 for distant-site telehealth beginning October 1 and instead bill the individual CPT or HCPCS service code with modifier 93 for qualifying real-time audio-only services or modifier 95 for real-time audio-video services. The CY 2026 payment rate is $97.53.

Why it matters: the policy shift creates denial risk across charge capture, coding, documentation, clearinghouses, and payer edits. A clinically valid telehealth encounter can still become a preventable revenue-cycle failure if workflows remain configured for G2025.
Executive question: Can every RHC demonstrate a successful end-to-end test claim under the new workflow before September 30?
4

Federal rural policy is funding both immediate stabilization and longer-term redesign

Rural PolicyContinuing

HHS and HRSA awarded nearly $25 million to 132 small rural hospitals across 13 states through the Rural Hospital Provider Assistance Program to support workforce and essential services. Separately, CMS Rural Health Transformation awards continue funding workforce, shared technology, regional coordination, and rural post-acute capacity.

Why it matters: federal policy is distinguishing between two needs: keeping fragile rural hospitals operating today and redesigning rural delivery systems for the future. Leaders should not confuse short-term stabilization dollars with a durable operating model.
Executive question: Which rural financial pressures require permanent payment reform, and which can realistically be solved through regional redesign, technology, or workforce investment?
5

Frontier AI safety pressure escalates as industry leaders call for independent evaluation

AI & CyberNew

Weekend reporting shows leading frontier-model executives calling for a slower, more coordinated approach to capability expansion, including independent evaluators with internal access, cross-company safety standards, and international coordination. The discussion follows recent evidence that advanced models can support sophisticated cyber, intelligence, and weapons-related tasks.

Why it matters: healthcare governance should assume that models with tools, credentials, or system access will become more capable faster than many enterprise control frameworks mature. Vendor assurances are not a substitute for independent logs, least-privilege access, human approval, and rapid shutdown capability.
Executive question: Are enterprise AI agents governed according to the authority they possess, with controls independent of the model and vendor?
