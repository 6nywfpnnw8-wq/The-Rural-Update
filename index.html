        CMS proposed rule CMS-2452-P would restrict provider-tax constructs that fund supplemental Medicaid payments

        Medicaid / Finance / Federal PolicyProposed

        CMS published a notice of proposed rulemaking (CMS-2452-P) amending indirect hold-harmless thresholds and clarifying permissible health-care-related tax constructs used to support supplemental Medicaid payments. The fact sheet cites an Office of the Actuary estimate of reduced federal outlays and lists statutory timing for some thresholds beginning Oct 1, 2026 and for indirect hold-harmless thresholds in most permissible classes beginning Oct 1, 2027.

        Why it matters: Hospitals that rely on state supplemental payments funded through provider-tax mechanisms face planning risk. Final rule language and state responses will determine whether material Medicaid revenue streams are reduced or require state redesign. Facility exposure depends on Oklahoma-specific provider-tax structures and OHCA decisions; do not rebase budgets without state documentation.
        Executive question: Which Oklahoma supplemental payments and provider-tax mechanisms fund material facility revenue, and how would those flows change if CMS finalizes the proposed restrictions?

        2

        RHC and FQHC distant-site telehealth billing conversion creates immediate revenue-cycle risk

        RHC / Revenue Cycle / MedicareFinal

        CMS transmittal R13776OTN and the CMS FQHC/RHC News page require RHCs and FQHCs to bill distant-site telehealth by individual CPT/HCPCS service codes plus applicable telehealth modifiers rather than the single G2025 code. CMS FQHC/RHC guidance states the policy applies to dates of service on or after October 1, 2026, while Transmittal R13776OTN lists an Implementation Date of October 5, 2026. These two dates serve different operational roles: the October 1, 2026 date is the policy dates-of-service effective date that sets the clinical and billing threshold for when providers should report services under the new code-and-modifier approach; the transmittal Implementation Date reflects the administrative/contractor processing date when CR/contractor materials and system changes described in the transmittal take effect. Revenue-cycle teams should treat October 1, 2026 as the billing date-of-service threshold for claims and proactively reconcile and submit claims accordingly, while monitoring carrier and contractor posting windows, EDI updates, and CR implementation schedules tied to the October 5, 2026 transmittal implementation window.

        Why it matters: Operational claim-format edits, telehealth modifier logic and payer EDI acceptance determine near-term cash flow. Denials or front-end rejections for Oct 1 onward telehealth dates of service can materially delay or reduce payments for previously G2025-billed visits. RHC revenue-cycle teams should reconcile production claims promptly to isolate payer-specific defects versus legitimate methodology changes.
        Executive question: Have our October telehealth claims using CPT/HCPCS plus telehealth modifiers been accepted and paid by Medicare and major commercial payers, and what denial patterns require immediate corrective edits?

        CMS announces GENEROUS Medicaid drug-payment model participants; Oklahoma execution must be verified

        Medicaid / Pharmacy / OklahomaAnnouncement

        CMS issued a press release describing national participation counts for the GENEROUS Medicaid drug-payment model and stating that all 50 states plus the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico applied; the release also stated that 40 states and Puerto Rico had signed agreements as of September 18, 2026. The press release does not provide a state-by-state executed-agreement list or confirm whether Oklahoma had executed participation terms. Before assuming Oklahoma has executed participation, obtain written confirmation from the Oklahoma Health Care Authority (OHCA) and request any state or MCO implementation terms that define product lists, rebate passthrough mechanics, or pharmacy operational instructions.

        Why it matters: If Oklahoma has executed participation agreements, pharmacy workflows, rebate passthrough instructions to MCOs and 340B implications could alter pharmacy revenue and formulary administration. A national participant list does not substitute for state-executed implementation terms; hospitals need OHCA confirmation before changing forecasts.
        Executive question: Has OHCA executed GENEROUS participation terms, and what are the product-level rebate, MCO pass-through and operational instructions that affect hospital pharmacy or 340B finances?

        5

        REH conversions continue to change local inpatient capacity and transfer patterns

        REH / Operations / OklahomaMonitoring

        CMS maintains REH certification, enrollment and oversight guidance that govern conversions from inpatient facilities to the Rural Emergency Hospital model, which preserves 24/7 emergency and outpatient services but discontinues inpatient and swing-bed services.

        Why it matters: Local REH conversions can remove inpatient and swing-bed revenue streams and increase transfers, EMS load and regional post-acute demand. Oklahoma hospitals should verify any reported local REH conversion with enrollment filings and state approvals before estimating financial impact.
        Executive question: Has our facility or nearby hospitals submitted CMS-855A change-of-information or REH enrollment forms, and what will be the operational and transfer-capacity implications for local EMS and post-acute care?
