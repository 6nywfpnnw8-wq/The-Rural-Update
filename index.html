Executive BLUF

Execution day: RHC/FQHC distant-site telehealth billing converts today from G2025 to individual CPT/HCPCS service codes with modifier 93 or 95 as applicable, and FY2027 ICD-10-CM/PCS codes are now effective. Oklahoma reports first-year Rural Health Transformation implementation is advancing, with the state emphasizing CMS spending milestones and SWOSU deploying a nearly $200,000 microgrant for mobile screening, chronic-disease prevention and telehealth in Washita and Roger Mills counties. CMS also announced $20 million for rural North Carolina digital infrastructure. The immediate management priority is verifying production claims, coding configuration and grant implementation rather than treating these changes as completed policy work.

Executive Actions

Today

Top 5 Developments

1

RHC telehealth billing conversion is live

ActionRevenue Cycle

For dates of service beginning October 1, RHCs and FQHCs must bill distant-site telehealth using the individual CPT or HCPCS service code and the appropriate revenue code and telehealth modifier instead of G2025. CMS specifies modifier 93 for qualifying audio-only services and modifier 95 for synchronous audio-video services. The CY2026 payment rate is $97.53.

Why it matters: the risk has moved from readiness to production claim performance.
Executive question: Are first claims clearing correctly, and is there a daily denial/rejection watch for the next two weeks?
2

Oklahoma RHTP moves deeper into implementation

OklahomaRural Health

Oklahoma’s September 30 progress report highlighted the state’s fifth-largest CMS RHTP award and continued focus on federal spending milestones. SWOSU is using a nearly $200,000 microgrant in Washita and Roger Mills counties for a mobile resource unit, screenings, chronic-disease prevention, telehealth and wellness equipment. Reporting from the Weatherford briefing says more than 95% of first-year funding has been obligated.

Why it matters: governance now needs to shift from award selection to implementation, outcomes and post-grant sustainability.
Executive question: Does every major project have an accountable owner, measurable outcome and recurring-cost plan?
3

FY2027 ICD-10 code sets take effect today

ComplianceRevenue Cycle

CMS and CDC FY2027 ICD-10-CM and ICD-10-PCS updates are effective for applicable discharges and encounters beginning October 1.

Why it matters: code-set mismatches can affect documentation, medical-necessity edits, claims, analytics and automated coding.
Executive question: Has end-to-end production readiness been verified beyond the vendor update?
4

North Carolina RHTP award reinforces digital infrastructure strategy

TechnologyRural Health

CMS announced $20 million for North Carolina’s Rural Health Innovation Fund to expand virtual care, upgrade electronic health records, strengthen cybersecurity and equip rural providers with new technology.

Why it matters: digital infrastructure is becoming core rural infrastructure, but subscriptions, support, connectivity and replacement cycles remain after grants end.
Executive question: Are recurring technology costs incorporated into post-grant sustainability tests?
5

Method II CAH line-level rendering provider requirements remain a revenue-cycle control

CAHMedicare

CMS requires Critical Access Hospitals billing Method II professional services to report rendering NPIs at the line level so Medicare can identify the professional associated with each outpatient service on a combined claim.

Why it matters: CAH combined billing depends on accurate provider-level claim data and clean EHR-to-claim mapping.
Executive question: Are Method II claims consistently carrying the correct rendering NPI at the service line?
