Top 5 Developments

1

AI coding moves into payer payment-integrity scrutiny

Revenue CycleAI

Blue Cross Blue Shield Association estimates that increased coding of medical complexity associated with hospital AI coding tools added $942 million in spending for Blue plans from 2023 through 2025. BCBSA reports that the share of inpatient cases coded as medically complex rose from 37% at the beginning of 2023 to 40% by the end of 2025, while its claims analysis found no evidence of a corresponding change in care delivered.

Why it matters: AI coding can legitimately improve documentation completeness, but payers now have a quantitative basis for stronger edits, audits and contract scrutiny. Governance needs to connect revenue cycle, compliance, clinical validation and managed care.
Executive question: Can the organization demonstrate that AI-associated increases in coding intensity are clinically supported and defensible under payer audit?
2

RHC telehealth conversion is six days away

Revenue CycleAction

On October 1, RHCs and FQHCs furnishing distant-site telehealth move from G2025 to individual CPT or HCPCS service codes, using modifier 93 for qualifying synchronous audio-only services and modifier 95 for synchronous audio-video services.

Why it matters: readiness now requires a clean claim across documentation, charge capture, coding, edits, clearinghouse transmission and payer processing. A residual legacy workflow can create avoidable denials.
Executive question: Can every RHC demonstrate a clean end-to-end test claim by September 30?
3

H-1B policy remains a rural recruitment cost risk

WorkforceFederal Policy

The administration renewed a $100,000 payment requirement affecting certain new H-1B petitions involving workers entering the United States. DHS separately proposes a $103,265 fee for cap-subject petitions. AHA is seeking a healthcare exemption and protection for foreign-trained physicians using Conrad 30 pathways.

Why it matters: six-figure immigration-related costs can make international recruitment uneconomic for small rural hospitals and practices even when a candidate is otherwise viable.
Executive question: Which rural vacancies depend on H-1B or Conrad 30 pathways, and what is the cost of losing those recruitment options?
4

RHTP reinforces local access plus regional capability

Rural HealthStrategy

CMS's September 24 Medicare learning update highlighted RHTP implementation in Connecticut, Missouri and New Mexico. The projects combine rural hospital infrastructure with regional behavioral and maternal specialty access, EMS workforce and regional hub organizations serving rural, frontier and Tribal communities.

Why it matters: the implementation pattern supports local hospitals as access points while scarce specialty, technology, workforce and data capabilities are shared regionally. The sustainability test remains whether those capabilities have a recurring payer after grants end.
Executive question: Which capabilities must remain local, which should be regional, and who pays the recurring operating cost in 2031?
5

Oklahoma Medicaid readiness approaches two near-term decision points

OklahomaMedicaid

September 30 is the deadline for remaining states to finalize CMS GENEROUS participation agreements. Separately, OHCA continues notifying potentially affected SoonerCare members about federal work requirements beginning January 1, 2027, generally requiring non-exempt adults ages 19 through 64 to meet 80 qualifying hours per month or the applicable income standard.

Why it matters: pharmacy-policy changes and eligibility churn can affect rural access, uncompensated care and clinic workflows at the same time Medicaid financing rules are tightening.
Executive question: Is Oklahoma's rural network ready to identify coverage disruption early and keep eligible patients connected to care?
