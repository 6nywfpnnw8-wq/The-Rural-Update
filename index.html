Top 5 Developments

1

Bipartisan REH 2.0 legislation would materially expand the Rural Emergency Hospital model

CongressAction

H.R. 10293, the Rural Emergency Hospital Designation Improvement Act, was introduced September 3 with bipartisan sponsorship and referred to the House Energy and Commerce and Ways and Means committees. The proposal broadens REH eligibility beyond a single fixed eligibility date and, according to the sponsors, would allow distinct inpatient psychiatric and obstetric units, provide dedicated laboratory funding, and clarify state Medicaid payment flexibility.

Why it matters: several of the design features hospitals have cited as barriers to REH conversion are directly targeted. The bill is not law, but it changes the strategic option set enough to justify re-screening facilities that previously ruled REH out.
Executive question: Which rural hospitals would become credible REH candidates if inpatient behavioral health or obstetric flexibility and stronger laboratory economics were available?
2

CY 2027 PFS comment deadline is six days away, with a direct RHC reimbursement opportunity

MedicareAction

CMS proposes treating Diabetes Self-Management Training and Medical Nutrition Therapy as qualified preventive services payable as stand-alone RHC visits through the All-Inclusive Rate. The proposed rule also addresses RHC/FQHC telehealth and Shared Savings Program policy. Comments close September 14.

Why it matters: the proposal offers a path to convert selected chronic-disease capability from grant-supported activity into recurring Medicare reimbursement, while broader physician payment and value-based provisions affect rural provider economics.
Executive question: Which chronic-disease services could become sustainable RHC offerings if the proposal is finalized, and what implementation work would be required?
3

OHCA's SoonerSelect Quality Advisory Committee meets today

OklahomaWatch

OHCA's Medicaid Delivery System Quality Advisory Committee meets September 8. The committee is charged by statute with evaluating quality performance and recommending measures for SoonerSelect contracted entities. Its 2026 work has included managed-care updates, external quality review, clinical-event operational metrics, performance-improvement projects, and primary-care spending.

Why it matters: SoonerSelect quality oversight is becoming a practical source of payer-performance intelligence. For rural providers, quality measures can influence administrative burden, network expectations, care-management workflows, and future value-based contracting.
Executive question: Which SoonerSelect quality measures or payer-performance indicators should be incorporated into rural affiliate scorecards rather than monitored only at the plan level?
4

Medicaid provider-tax comments close September 21, keeping 2028 to 2031 financing risk in focus

MedicaidAction

CMS-2452-P would implement statutory restrictions on new or increased healthcare-related taxes and revise the indirect hold-harmless test. CMS's Office of the Actuary estimates the proposed implementation would reduce federal Medicaid expenditures by $246 billion from 2026 through 2035.

Why it matters: provider taxes, state-directed payments, DSH, base reimbursement, and uncompensated care operate as one financing system. A facility-level model that isolates any one component risks understating the combined downside.
Executive question: Which rural facilities have the least capacity to absorb a simultaneous decline in supplemental Medicaid financing and increase in uncompensated care?
5

Frontier AI has crossed a new cybersecurity capability threshold

AI & TechnologyWatch

OpenAI states that GPT-6 Astra is its first broadly deployed model to reach the Critical cybersecurity capability level under its Preparedness Framework. The company says the model can, with appropriate tools and access, find previously unknown vulnerabilities and develop new exploits across protected systems without step-by-step human guidance, prompting stronger isolation, monitoring, and containment controls.

Why it matters: healthcare organizations should assume AI will accelerate both defensive vulnerability discovery and offensive capability. Agentic systems with network or application permissions should therefore be governed like privileged identities, with containment and revocation controls.
Executive question: Can the organization identify, isolate, and revoke every AI agent's access independently, and are its logs outside the agent's control?
