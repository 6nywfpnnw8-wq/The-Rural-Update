Executive BLUF

The immediate decision point is tomorrow's September 21 deadline for CMS-2452-P provider-tax comments. The rural downside case should now include both permanent Medicaid financing pressure and liquidity stress from delayed supplemental payments. The broader strategic issue remains post-grant sustainability: KFF estimates the $50 billion RHTP equals about 37% of estimated ten-year rural Medicaid reductions, with most of those reductions occurring after 2030. October 1 remains the operational deadline for the RHC telehealth billing conversion. A fresh digital-health privacy development also matters: the FTC withdrew an obsolete 2021 health-app policy statement because its 2024 Health Breach Notification Rule amendments now directly cover health apps and connected devices.

Top 5 Developments

1

Medicaid provider-tax deadline is tomorrow

MedicaidAction

Comments on CMS-2452-P close September 21. The proposal implements statutory restrictions on health-care-related provider taxes and revised indirect hold-harmless thresholds. The Texas supplemental-payment dispute this month also demonstrated that Medicaid financing exposure includes payment timing and liquidity, not only permanent revenue reductions.

Why it matters: rural hospitals can face financing pressure, coverage churn and delayed cash at the same time. Annual-margin modeling alone is inadequate.
Executive question: Does the downside case quantify permanent revenue exposure plus 30-, 60- and 90-day supplemental-payment delays?
2

RHTP sustainability remains the central strategic test

Rural Health

KFF's September 18 analysis estimates that the $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program equals about 37% of estimated ten-year federal Medicaid reductions in rural areas and that 64% of those reductions occur after 2030.

Why it matters: temporary transformation funding can establish capability, but recurring staffing, software, maintenance and replacement costs remain after grant support ends.
Executive question: Which investments remain viable in 2031 with zero grant subsidy and a less favorable Medicaid environment?
3

RHC telehealth conversion is 11 days away

Revenue CycleAction

On October 1, RHCs and FQHCs furnishing distant-site telehealth move from G2025 to individual CPT or HCPCS service codes, using modifier 93 for qualifying audio-only services and modifier 95 for synchronous audio-video services.

Why it matters: this is an end-to-end revenue-cycle conversion across documentation, charge capture, coding, edits and clearinghouses.
Executive question: Can every RHC demonstrate a clean test claim before September 30?
4

Health-app privacy obligations now rest on the updated FTC rule

Digital Health

The FTC withdrew its 2021 Health Breach Notification Rule policy statement as obsolete, explaining that its 2024 rule amendments now expressly cover health apps and connected devices. The updated rule treats certain unauthorized disclosures, not only cyber intrusions, as breaches requiring notification.

Why it matters: health systems increasingly connect patients to apps, wearables and digital-health vendors that may sit outside HIPAA. Vendor governance must account for FTC obligations and downstream data sharing.
Executive question: Do digital-health reviews identify where patient data leaves HIPAA-regulated workflows and which party owns breach-notification obligations?
5

Oklahoma's long-acting OUD opportunity remains implementation-dependent

OklahomaBehavioral Health

OHCA's removal of prior authorization for Brixadi reduces a payer barrier to weekly or monthly injectable buprenorphine for SoonerCare members, with particular potential relevance to rural and underserved communities.

Why it matters: coverage alone does not create access. Rural clinics still need prescribing, administration, counseling, monitoring and referral workflows.
Executive question: Which rural clinics can operate a dependable long-acting buprenorphine pathway?
