Executive BLUF

CMS announced a new $20 million Rural Health Transformation investment for North Carolina focused on telehealth, electronic health records, cybersecurity and rural provider technology, extending the pattern of RHTP dollars being used to build distributed access and digital infrastructure. HRSA Rural Hospital Provider Assistance awards also begin today for eligible small rural hospitals with no more than 50 acute beds and a Medicare wage index below 0.90, reinforcing federal attention to hospitals with structural payment and workforce disadvantages. In Oklahoma, FY2026 Rural Communities Opioid Response awards include McAlester Regional Health Center Authority, Cimarron Memorial Hospital & Nursing Home, the Peoria Tribe and Lift Community Action Agency. The immediate operational priority is tomorrow’s RHC/FQHC telehealth billing conversion from G2025 to individual CPT/HCPCS codes.

Executive Actions

Today
Close the RHC telehealth readiness item. Confirm coding, modifiers, charge capture, edits and payer transmission before tomorrow’s conversion.
Q4
Review Oklahoma RCORP connectivity. Determine whether affiliate hospitals and RHCs can connect to McAlester, Cimarron, Peoria Tribe or Lift Community Action Agency initiatives.
Q4
Use current RHTP awards as a sustainability benchmark. Require recurring cost, staffing, reimbursement and replacement-capital assumptions for technology and mobile-care investments.

Top 5 Developments

1

North Carolina RHTP award targets telehealth and rural technology

Rural HealthTechnology

CMS announced a $20 million Rural Health Transformation investment for North Carolina to expand virtual care, upgrade electronic health records, strengthen cybersecurity and equip rural providers with new technology.

Why it matters: the award reinforces the emerging RHTP pattern of using temporary capital to build distributed access and digital infrastructure rather than only brick-and-mortar capacity.
Executive question: Which technology investments have a sustainable operating model after grant funding ends?
2

HRSA rural hospital assistance awards begin today

FinanceWorkforce

FY2026 Rural Hospital Provider Assistance awards begin September 30. The competition targets eligible rural hospitals with no more than 50 acute inpatient beds and Medicare wage index values below 0.90, with projects supporting provider capacity and essential services.

Why it matters: the program is aimed directly at structural rural disadvantages tied to low wage indices, thin workforce markets and fragile service lines.
Executive question: Which affiliate facilities have comparable structural risks even if they were not selected in this cohort?
3

Oklahoma receives new rural opioid-response awards

OklahomaBehavioral Health

HRSA’s FY2026 Rural Communities Opioid Response awards include McAlester Regional Health Center Authority, Cimarron Memorial Hospital & Nursing Home, the Peoria Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma and Lift Community Action Agency across overdose response, planning and impact tracks.

Why it matters: the awards create new regional behavioral-health and substance-use infrastructure that rural hospitals and RHCs may be able to connect with rather than duplicate.
Executive question: Where can affiliate facilities establish referral, medication-treatment, recovery or care-coordination relationships with these awardees?
4

RHC telehealth billing conversion starts tomorrow

ActionRevenue Cycle

Beginning October 1, RHCs and FQHCs must bill distant-site telehealth using the individual CPT or HCPCS code for the service and the applicable telehealth modifier rather than G2025. CMS lists modifier 93 for qualifying audio-only services and modifier 95 for synchronous audio-video services.

Why it matters: readiness now depends on the full revenue-cycle chain, not just coding education.
Executive question: Can every affected clinic demonstrate a clean end-to-end claim before close of business today?
5

Montana award highlights rural EMS as core access infrastructure

EMSAccess

CMS announced $8.7 million for 79 rural Montana EMS agencies to replace aging ambulances and purchase equipment including ventilators and cardiac monitors.

Why it matters: rural hospital strategy increasingly depends on transport capacity, particularly where REH conversion or regionalization moves inpatient and specialty care farther from patients.
Executive question: Are EMS capacity and capital needs being modeled alongside hospital service redesign?
